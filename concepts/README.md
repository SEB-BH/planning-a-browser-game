## Turn a large project into smaller decisions

“Build a game” sounds like one task, but it contains many decisions.

A player needs to understand the rules, make a move, see what happens, and know when the game ends.

Planning helps us identify those smaller pieces before we begin coding.

## A first look at Scrum

**Scrum** is a framework that helps teams organize their work and improve a product over time.

Teams work in fixed periods called **sprints**. During a sprint, they work toward a goal. They then review the result and use what they learn to plan their next steps. [Scrum Guides](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html)

For this individual project, we will borrow a few of these ideas:

1. List the work.
2. Choose a small group of features.
3. Build and test them.
4. Review the result before choosing the next group.

> 💡 This is an introduction to working in small steps. We will explore the full Scrum framework in Unit 2.

## Decide what your game needs first

**MVP** means **minimum viable product**.

For this project, it means:

> The smallest playable version of your game that meets all the project requirements.

For Tic-Tac-Toe, our **MVP** is the smallest complete version that allows two people to play a full game.

| Needed for the MVP | Could come later |
| ------------------ | ---------------- |
| Instructions explaining the rules | A choice of visual themes |
| A board with nine squares | Custom player names |
| Two players taking turns as X and O | Playing against the computer |
| Preventing moves in occupied squares | Sound effects |
| Detecting a win or draw | Animated celebrations |
| Displaying the result and stopping further moves | A scoreboard that tracks several rounds |
| A way to play again | Saving results after closing the browser |
